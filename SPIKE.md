# SPIKE — Bermuda Triangle (working title)

> **Logline:** Transport Fever 2, but you're the extraterrestrials running Earth as a
> logistics colony — from lowly cattle-abduction milk runs to interstellar cruise liners,
> all while keeping humanity from finding out.

**Status:** concept riff. Nothing built. This doc captures the design space.

---

## Does it exist?

**No** — not this exact thing. Adjacent but mechanically different:

- **Destroy All Humans!** — you're an alien abducting cattle/humans, Area 51, 50s vibe — but it's third-person *action*, not a logistics sim.
- **XCOM / Phoenix Point** — alien invasion, but you play *defense*.
- **OpenTTD / Transport Fever / Mini Motorways** — the routing-loop DNA, but human + terrestrial.

The cattle-mutilation → interstellar-tourism progression on a nested planetary globe is open territory.

---

## The core hook (what makes it a new game, not a reskin)

Transport Fever's negative externality is **pollution**. Ours is **EXPOSURE**.

- Every route radiates risk. Daylight saucer over a city = witnesses, photos, radar paint. Night run over open ocean = clean.
- A global **Disclosure Meter** fills with exposure. Fill it → governments wise up → jets scramble, corridors patrolled, bases raided → lose.
- This adds a **third optimization axis** beyond TF2's fuel/time/capacity: **how invisible is the run?** That axis is the whole game.

**Mitigation tech tree:** cloaking → memory-wipe payloads → Men-in-Black suppression → disinformation (flood the net with *fake* footage so real footage gets buried).

## Supply chain (TF2 loop, reskinned)

| TF2 | Bermuda Triangle |
|---|---|
| Coal / iron | Cattle (biomass), abductees (test subjects), seawater, atmospheric gas, soil cores |
| Factory | Mothership labs / Dulce processing / biomass vats |
| Goods | Genetic samples, "experience footage," protein slurry, refined Element-115 |
| Town that grows | **Patron civilizations** that grow as you feed their cravings |

**Buyer factions** (the "towns"), each addicted to a different output:

- **Zeta Reticuli** → genetic material (abductee runs)
- **Pleiadians** → tourism; they pay to be *brought here* to gawk (passenger branch)
- **Reptilians** → raw biomass at industrial scale (cattle freight)
- **Galactic Federation** → the *regulator*; enforces the masquerade, fines you as Disclosure climbs (Prime Directive as a compliance system)

## The Race Hierarchy (three mechanics in one)

The lore hands us a literal **vertical caste structure**. We make it carry triple duty:
it's the **progression ladder**, the **crew-allocation system**, and it **couples directly to exposure**.

### The Pyramid — and you start at the bottom

The hierarchy *is* the tech tree. You don't "research tier 2" — you get **promoted up the
galactic caste system.** You begin as a lowly Grey-tier subcontractor doing cattle milk runs and
climb, each rung granting authority over the races below + better craft, fuel, and contracts.

| Race | Tier | Mechanical role | Vibe |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Draco / Reptilian elite** | Aristocracy — owns the franchise | Quota-setters & the "go loud" occupation patron. Your bosses, or a rival bidding for Earth | Ruthless, extractive |
| **Mantids (insectoid)** | Directors / science authority | Run the labs; **gate-keep high-value outputs** (genetic, "consciousness" data). Rare, required, unsettling | Clinical, revered |
| **Tall Greys** | Operational management | Base supervisors → +throughput, **−exposure** on crews they oversee | Bureaucratic |
| **Short Greys (Zeta)** | Labor drones | Crew the saucers, do the abductions. Cheap, expendable, **sloppy (exposure+)** | Hive drone |
| **Nordics / Pleiadians** | *Off to the side / above it* | Benevolent counter-faction: tourism + "uplift" contracts. Low-exposure, high-margin, slow | Luxe, ethical |

Nordics sitting *outside* the pyramid is the key bit — they're the **moral/strategic alternative**, not labor.

### Alignment axis (couples to the Disclosure meter)

**Reptilian/extractive** ←──────────→ **Nordic/benevolent**

- **Draco side:** high volume, raw biomass, occupation. Fat profits, *Disclosure spikes hard.* The "drop the masquerade" endgame is the Reptilian agenda winning.
- **Nordic side:** tourism, observation, careful contact. Lower throughput, premium margins, *keeps Disclosure flat.*

Race choices **tune the exposure dial directly** — the hierarchy and the soul-of-the-game meter are the same system viewed two ways.

### Fractal crew allocation

Same risk/reward shape at the per-craft micro level. Staff each saucer from the labor pool:

- **All-Grey crew** → cheap, fast to field, **sloppy → exposure penalty** (your blurry-photo incidents).
- **+ Tall Grey supervisor** → costs more, cleaner runs.
- **+ Mantid aboard** → required to harvest premium genetic/consciousness outputs, but scarce and you answer to them.

"Who's flying this saucer" becomes a real per-route decision — fractal design, always good.

### Humans aren't the obstacle — they're a *layer inside* the hierarchy

The Greada-treaty mythology hands this to us for free:

- **MJ-12 / breakaway government** — humans you've **cut a deal with.** Hand over tech; they suppress Disclosure & look away on abductions. A *cover supplier*: spend a "tech-transfer budget" to keep the lid on. Stiff them → they leak.
- **MiB / human assets** — recruit human operatives for cheap *ground-level* suppression (cheaper than scrambling craft for every witness).
- **Hybrids** — the hybridization program as a **production chain**: abductees + genetic material → hybrids → a class that **passes as human (near-zero ground exposure).** Your stealth infiltration agents.
- **Disclosure movement** — antagonist pressure that **grows with your activity.** Main event-generator; the thing MJ-12 helps you tamp down.

Humans span collaborator → asset → manufactured-class → antagonist. Woven through every tier, not bolted on the side.

## Vehicles (bespoke catalog, TF2-style stat cards)

Stat card: capacity / speed / fuel use / upkeep / era / manufacturer + **exposure footprint**.

- **Pills / foo-fighters** — tiny fast probes, recon. The "horse and cart" opener.
- **Scout saucers** — workhorse. Modular: *livestock*, *medical/abductee*, *sample* variants. The boxcar.
- **Black triangles (TR-3B)** — heavy lift, slow, but **low exposure** (witnesses assume it's human military). Hiding in plain sight as a stat.
- **Cigars / Tic-Tacs** — long-haul cruisers. The freight train.
- **Motherships** — passenger branch. Pleiadian cruise tours w/ observation decks, comfort rating, itinerary stops (Giza → Vegas → Nazca → Burning Man). This is the literal passenger-car vs storage-car split.

**Manufacturers** (flavor + stat identity): Grey Industries (cheap, efficient, ugly) · Nordic Luxury Liners (passenger, pricey, gorgeous) · Reptilian Heavy Works (max capacity, max exposure, guzzlers).

## Fuel = eras = progression spine

Map fuel tiers onto real UFO-lore history so the tech tree *is* the secret history:

1. **1947–50s** — red mercury / antigrav prototypes. Short intra-continental hops. Cattle milk runs. (Roswell crash = tutorial → first base.)
2. **Cold War** — **Element 115** reactors (Bob Lazar). Orbital + lunar reach.
3. **70s–90s** — zero-point / vacuum energy. Interplanetary within the solar system.
4. **Modern → future** — exotic matter / Casimir drives. Stable wormholes → interstellar (Pleiades, Kepler-b, Zeta).

Each fuel has its own supply chain + refineries — like coal/oil depots in TF2, you also haul **fuel to depots**.

## Map: nested-zoom globe (signature visual)

Not a flat terrain map — a planet you wrap routes around, nesting outward:

`State→State (US)` → `Continent→Continent` → `Earth↔Orbit` → `Cislunar` → `Solar System` → `Local stars` → `Galactic`

Each tier unlocks with fuel tech. "Texas→New Mexico cattle hop" to "Catalina→Kepler-b" on one continuous zoomable globe = the trailer shot.

**Bases (stations, upgradable):**
Roswell (tutorial) · Area 51 / S4 (R&D + assembly, unlocks craft) · Catalina & Bermuda (oceanic ports + natural wormhole nodes) · Dulce (genetics lab) · Cerro Escondido (LatAm cattle hub) · Antarctica (late-game hollow-earth megabase).

## The open design problem (must solve)

TF2's joy verb is **laying track / terraforming** — drawing infrastructure. Point-to-point saucers on a globe risk collapsing into a spreadsheet of routes.

**Fix — what you physically build/draw:**

- **The network:** bases, orbital waystations, relay beacons, **wormhole gates** (the "bridges/tunnels"). Bermuda Triangle = free *natural* gate, unstable. Built gates = expensive but reliable.
- **Flight corridors** you hand-draw and tune across the exposure/fuel/time triangle — hug coastlines, dive through vortices, dodge radar.

The buildable infra is the **stealth-and-wormhole network**; corridor-drawing replaces track-laying. Nail this or it's just a dashboard.

## Events / spice

- **Crop circles** = free advertising → spikes tourist demand.
- Famous abductee **publishes a book** → Disclosure spike to suppress.
- **Wormhole misfire** → cattle saucer drops into 1692 Salem (time-displacement side mission; or sell a stolen dinosaur to a museum-world).
- **President almost discloses** on live TV → emergency MiB timer.
- **Endgame branch — drop the masquerade:** go full *Independence Day*. Retool the chain for open occupation/harvest. New demands, new vehicles, no exposure cap. A real "stay hidden vs go loud" identity choice.

## Why Disclosure = losing (making the stakes non-arbitrary)

The trap question: you're a superior civilization — why fear humans finding out? The answer is
*economic and legal*, not military. A Grey fears a farmer about as much as you fear the cow.

1. **Earth is a licensed, secret concession under galactic law.** The masquerade isn't a
   preference — it's the core clause of your harvesting permit. The Federation doesn't punish
   *humanity* for finding out; it punishes **you** for getting caught: license revoked, assets
   seized, blacklisted. The regulator we already have *is* the executioner.
2. **The goods are only valuable while humanity is unaware** — the "free-range" model. An observed
   harvest is a spoiled harvest: a humanity that *knows* panics, militarizes, performs. The
   genetic authenticity, tourist experiences, and consciousness data all derive worth from humans
   behaving naturally and unobserved. Disclose → your whole product line craters (tourism dies
   first — nobody pays to watch animals looking back).
3. **Disclosure forfeits your monopoly.** Once Earth is "revealed" as a known inhabited resource
   world, rivals and the Federation auction off your exclusive claim. Secrecy kept it *yours*.

**The payoff:** disclosure is only a loss when it happens *to* you. If you're strong enough you can
*choose* to cross the line on your terms — the **Draco "go loud" occupation** endgame. Same
threshold: trip it and lose, detonate it deliberately and win. That's the two-endgame fork.

The Disclosure Meter is a spectrum, not a switch: rumors (manageable) → government awareness
(interference, rising costs, MJ-12 strain) → public disclosure (license revoked → loss).

## The core verb: transit authoring, not track-laying (resolved)

The open design problem above — "where's the track-laying joy?" — resolves to **bus-stop wiring**.
World nodes already exist; you don't build the world, you wire it: pick a source stop, pick a
destination, an arc appears, a craft auto-shuttles the loop forever, currency trickles in. The
depth lives in *which* stops you connect, *which corridor* the arc takes (the exposure/distance
tradeoff), and *which craft* you assign — plus the wormhole-gate + stealth network you unlock later.
Prototyped in the app at `/prototype` (Level 1, Roswell), including a playable Disclosure loss-state.

## Cold open: the Roswell inheritance (tutorial framing)

The campaign opens *after* a failure, not at zero. **Your predecessor crashed at Roswell in 1947
and nearly blew the masquerade** — the real-world "weather balloon" story is the cover-up that
barely held. You're the **new operator** sent to clean up: you inherit a half-exposed operation
with an **already-elevated Exposure baseline**, and your first job is to operate *quietly* enough
that it decays back down. This is the perfect tutorial: it teaches the Disclosure meter from the
first second, with built-in motivation ("don't be the next guy"), and it canonizes the most famous
UFO event as your origin story. Mechanically: start Level 1 with Exposure ~24%; laying low lowers
it, overreach trips it. Implemented in `/prototype`.

## The promotion ladder (hierarchy = campaign spine)

Each rung is an *era* (fuel + map zoom) **and** a *caste rank* (authority + craft). Climbing is the through-line.

1. **Subcontractor (Grey labor)** — 1947–50s. Cattle milk runs, intra-continental, red-mercury hops. Prove you can move biomass without ending up on a front page.
2. **Crew Chief (Tall Grey sign-off)** — Cold War. Element-115 unlocked, orbital reach, abductee program begins. First exposure crises.
3. **Lab-Sanctioned Operation (Mantid patronage)** — 70s–90s. Premium genetic/consciousness outputs, interplanetary, hybrid production comes online.
4. **Franchise Holder** — modern→future. **The fork:** pledge to the **Draco** (extractive, occupation, exposure uncapped) or the **Nordics** (tourism, uplift, stealth). Wormholes → interstellar.
5. **First Contact / Disclosure** — the two win-states: orchestrate *controlled* disclosure on your terms (Nordic), or *conquest* logistics at planetary scale (Draco). Lose-state: humanity discloses *you* first.

## Replayability & customization (escaping the script)

**Core principle:** scripted = one optimal path; replayable = the optimal path *changes every run*.
You don't get there with more content — you get there by making *starting conditions × build choices
× self-set goals* combine into a different puzzle each time. **The promotion ladder above is the
script — and that's fine as *onboarding* only.** Run 1 rides the rail (Roswell → climb); runs 2–200
live in the sandbox below. Levers, strongest first:

1. **Asymmetric factions = the #1 lever.** Turn Grey / Nordic / Mantid / Reptilian / MJ-12 from
   flavor into *full alternate rulesets*. Grey = cheap sloppy volume; Nordic = tourism-only, no
   abduction, hyper-stealth premium; Mantid = genetics research-rush; Reptilian = extractive,
   exposure-uncapped occupation; MJ-12 = play *through* humans (bribery/cover, minimal flights).
   Different vehicles, currencies, win conditions, failure modes → effectively 5 games. (Civ model.)
2. **Seeded world-state.** Earth's geography is fixed, but the layer above it is per-seed: resource
   deposits (cattle / uranium / volcanic / hybrid-rich populations), which governments' MJ-12
   cooperate vs are hostile, where the disclosure movement is strong, which famous cases are *live*
   this run, rival faction claims, starting era/crash site. Same planet, different pressure map.
3. **The galaxy is procedural.** Earth = authored tutorial-world; exoplanets/star systems generate
   procedurally for an endless late game. Crafted head, infinite tail.
4. **Build customization with opportunity cost.** Modular saucers (cargo/cloak/passenger/medical
   bays), base specialization, branching tech you can't fully complete, crew composition
   (Grey/Tall-Grey/Mantid cost-vs-exposure), and player-authored network topology (the OpenTTD
   signature — no two route graphs alike).
5. **Player-authored goals + mutators + scoring.** Reward many playstyles (max biomass / max
   stealth / disclosure speedrun / pacifist tourism / ironman). Setup mutators: scarce fuel,
   aggressive disclosure, hostile MJ-12, "smartphone era = hard."
6. **Emergent crises, not scripted missions.** Events fire from system state (exposure threshold →
   sighting flap; over-serve Reptilians → Nordic sanctions; stiff a government → raid). The story is
   generated by your choices interacting — never the same beats twice.
7. **Multiple asymmetric endings.** Replay to *see a different ending*: controlled disclosure,
   tourism monopoly, full occupation, genetic singularity, or ascend the hierarchy to Director.
8. **Mod / Workshop support.** Genre lifeblood (TF2/OpenTTD) — custom vehicles, maps, scenarios.

**The non-negotiable:** no dominant strategy (Sid Meier's "series of interesting decisions"). The
best build must depend on the seed + faction, or it's just a script with extra steps.

### The level openness curve (tutorial → sandbox)

Linearity isn't a switch — it's five dials turned a little each level. The hand-holding in L1 is the
*contract* that earns the right to make the sandbox merciless.

| Level | Scale | New system | Objectives | Guidance | Lose? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **L1 Roswell** | one region | the verb + exposure + faction pick | scripted, step-by-step | heavy (forced camera, lit nodes) | barely |
| **L2 Continental** | a continent | processing chains (ranch→lab→patron) + contracts w/ deadlines | goals, not clicks | camera freed | yes |
| **L3 Global** | whole globe | rival faction + terrain/day-night exposure + vehicle classes | you set strategy | nodes un-highlighted | readily |
| **L4 Orbital** | solar system | fuel logistics + wormhole gates + the Nordic/Draco fork | self-directed | none | constantly |
| **Sandbox** | galaxy (procedural) | seeded world-state, all factions, mutators | none — you invent them | none | merciless |

**Mechanism:** each level *widens the map, stacks one system, converts directives → goals, removes a
layer of guidance, and raises lose-pressure.* By L3 you're off the rails; the campaign is scaffolding
you climb and kick away.

**Faction pick** (the asymmetry lever) is prototyped in Level 1 at `/prototype`: pick **Grey**
(volume — yield 9, exposure ×1.35, ranches, Biomass) or **Nordic** (tourism — yield 7, exposure
×0.32, landmarks, Footage). Same map, different game. You commit to one as your identity; the others
remain in the world as patrons & rivals.
