# Level 2 — Continental (the first rails-off level)

The bridge out of the tutorial. Level 1 taught the verb on one region with heavy guidance; Level 2
**removes the rails and stacks one tier of systems** (see the openness curve in `SPIKE.md`). You are
now **Crew Chief**, not a subcontractor — and the map, the chains, and the clock all widen.

---

## Premise

You passed License Renewal; the Federation grants you a continent. Element-115 reactors come online
(orbital hops are now possible), and the patrons stop hand-feeding you single routes — they post a
*live, competitive* board and expect you to run a *network*, not a line.

---

## What's new (one tier up on each dial)

| Dial | Level 1 | Level 2 |
|---|---|---|
| **Scale** | one region (US SW) | the whole continent + first **orbital** hops |
| **New system** | the verb + exposure | **processing chains** + **deadlines** + a **rival operator** |
| **Objectives** | scripted, step-by-step | a live board you *choose* from; no forced order |
| **Guidance** | forced camera, lit nodes | none — you read the map yourself |
| **Lose pressure** | barely | real (rival can out-bid you; Disclosure bites) |

### 1. Processing chains (the headline addition)

Routes stop being `source → base`. Value now flows through **multi-hop chains**:

```
Ranch (biomass) ──▶ Dulce (lab: refine) ──▶ Patron
Abductee ──▶ Dulce (genome) ──▶ Area 51 (consciousness data) ──▶ Mantid
```

Each hop is its own route with its own craft/crew/corridor and its own exposure. A chain is only as
quiet as its loudest leg — so the optimization problem goes 2-D. Labs gain throughput caps and queues
(a backed-up lab spoils perishable cargo, tying into `02`'s decay deadlines).

### 2. A rival operator

A **competing alien franchise** (Reptilian-aligned by default) bids on the same contracts and harvests
the same sites. Mechanics: contested sites deplete faster; the rival's sloppy runs raise *regional*
Disclosure you didn't cause (shared masquerade); you can sabotage them (raises your exposure) or let
the Federation catch them (frees their claims). First taste of **competition**, not just management.

### 3. Deadlines & the live board

Contracts now expire on visible timers and arrive faster than you can serve them — you triage. Perishable
cargo (genome, specimens) adds hard clocks. This is where the **route inspector** (`07`) earns its keep:
you're balancing 4–6 chains against the audit clock.

### 4. Fuel logistics (light touch)

Element-115 must be *refined and hauled to depots* (Los Alamos → bases). Orbital hops need it. This is
the seed of the Level 3+ fuel economy; in L2 it's a single new supply line you must not let run dry.

## Map & regions

Three linked regions, each with its own Exposure pool (Exposure goes *regional* here, per `05`'s
note): **Pacific** (Catalina, Hollywood), **Heartland** (Roswell, ranches, Dulce, Area 51),
**Atlantic/Gulf** (Shag Harbour, Bermuda Vortex, Cape Canaveral — new). The **Bermuda Vortex** becomes
a usable shortcut between Atlantic and orbit. New sites: **Cape Canaveral** (ride human rocket noise as
cover), **Mexico City / Cerro Escondido** (LatAm staging), **Wright-Patterson** (Federation HQ — the
audit happens here now, in person).

---

## New mechanics summary

- **Regional Exposure** (3 pools) — heat is now local; a blown region can be quarantined while you
  work the others.
- **Lab queues & throughput** — processing is a constraint, not a free transform.
- **Rival franchise** — contested claims, shared-masquerade Disclosure spillover.
- **Element-115 supply line** — keep depots fed or lose orbital reach.
- **Standing tiers** — Crew Chief perks; the Nordic/Draco fork **opens** (you can now formally pledge).

---

## Win / lose

- **Win:** hit the Federation's continental quota *and* survive the L2 renewal with Disclosure under a
  (tighter) cap — promotes to **Lab-Sanctioned Operator** (Level 3: global + the alignment fork commits).
- **Lose:** Disclosure Event, *or* the rival out-competes you to a Federation buyout of your claims
  (a new, non-Disclosure loss — you're simply outperformed).

---

## How it removes rails (the through-line)

L1 told you *which* node to click; L2 hands you a board and a rival and says *figure out the network*.
The camera no longer flies you anywhere, nodes aren't highlighted, and the "right" build now depends on
the rival's moves and the contract mix — the first genuinely **open** state. The tutorial scaffolding is
gone; what remains is the systemic game the whole spike has been building toward.
